

El electro nace en Detroit, Nueva York y Los Ángeles, a principios de los 80's en las escenas urbanas y de clubes de baile underground, fuertemente influenciado por el funk y la música electrónica europea. Se caracteriza por su patrón de batería sincopado (ritmo quebrado o breakbeat), marcado de forma robótica por cajas de ritmos clásicas como la Roland TR-808. Se mantiene en un rango de 120 y 140 BPM.


El trance nace en Fráncfort y Berlín (Alemania), a principios de los 90's en los clubes underground europeos, evolucionando a partir del techno, el ambient y el acid house. Se caracteriza por su estructura de tensión y liberación (build-ups y drops), marcada por melodías hipnóticas de sintetizador y acordes repetitivos sobre un bombo rítmico continuo de 4/4. Se mantiene en un rango de 128 y 150 BPM.

El techno es un género de música electrónica nacido en Detroit en los años 80, caracterizado por un sonido repetitivo, mecánico y un ritmo constante. A lo largo de los años, ha dado lugar a varios subgéneros con diferentes velocidades, texturas y atmósferas.

Esta es nuestra misión desde 1994.
Time Warp se consolidó en 1994 como uno de los mayores espectáculos de techno del mundo.

La edición alemana del festival atrae a miles de personas cada año. Desde el año 2000, Mannheim es la sede del festival.

Durante tres décadas, Time Warp se ha distinguido a nivel mundial por su impecable producción.

Time Warp tuvo su primera edición el 26 de noviembre de 1994 en la ciudad de Ludwigshafen, Alemania. Fue concebido por Steffen Charles y Cosmopop con un propósito claro: contrarrestar la comercialización de la música electrónica de la época y ofrecer un espacio puro para el techno underground.
Aunque comenzó en un antiguo molino de granos (Walzmühle), para el año 2000 el festival encontró su hogar definitivo en el Maimarkthalle en Mannheim, Alemania, un complejo industrial que se convirtió en la catedral del festival.


Lineup report
Scope and reading note. The genre field below uses four practical dominant-style groupings: Techno, House/Indie Dance, Electro, and Trance/Hard Dance. These are not exclusive genre claims. The three songs under each artist are a reasoned selection of important or representative work, not an objective ranking, and a recorded DJ set is identified as such where the documented discography is unusually small.

1. Alarico b2b Ben Klock — Techno
A back-to-back booking, not a permanent duo: Alarico brings fast, percussive hardgroove/raw-techno energy, while Klock supplies deeper, hypnotic, industrial and narrative Berlin techno. 1

	•	Alarico (Alarico Zucchi) — Nationality: Italian. Birth: reported born in 1999 in Italy; exact day and city are not verified. Milan is his established base/origin, not necessarily his birthplace. Impact: A notable younger voice in the hardgroove/raw-techno revival, linking 1990s rhythm science with hip-hop and Japanese-cultural references through Katana Records, Mutual Rytm and Klockworks. Key songs: AF 97; Boya; Drippin’ Blood.
	•	Ben Klock — Nationality: German. Birth: 1972, Schöneberg, Germany; a complete day-month date is not reliably published. Impact: A central architect and international ambassador of the Berghain/Ostgut-era sound, combining industrial weight, groove, melody, marathon DJ narrative and Klockworks label curation. Key songs: Subzero; Dawning; Phantom Studies.

2. Andy Martin — Techno
This resolves to the Guadalajara-based Mexican/Jamaican electronic artist, not the separate US jazz trombonist. The label means dub/leftfield/experimental techno as a center of gravity. 3

	•	Andy Martin — Nationality: Mexican, with Jamaican heritage; the sources do not establish dual citizenship. Birth: exact date is not documented; Resident Advisor gives Mexico as the birth country. Guadalajara is his location, not a verified birthplace. Impact: Bridges Jamaican soundsystem/dub practice, contemporary techno, Latin-Caribbean rhythm and Mexican literary, artistic and pre-Hispanic-futurist narratives; Mole Audio and Diaspora Echoes extend that work beyond his own records, while the Lee Scratch Perry collaboration shows cross-generational reach. Key songs: Revolution; Pedro Paramo (Original Mix); The Gurdjieff Movements.

3. Anfisa Letyago — Techno
A solo artist whose Neapolitan, groove-led techno also absorbs melodic, vocal, house and electronic-pop textures. 5

	•	Anfisa Letyago — Nationality: Russian-born and Italy-based, strongly identified with Naples; sources do not establish dual legal citizenship. 4. The Blessed Madonna — House/Indie Dance
Marea Renee Stamper is the former Black Madonna; this is one artist, not a B2B. Her core vocabulary is Chicago/acid house, disco and emotionally expansive club music, with techno, electro and new-wave crossover. 7

	•	The Blessed Madonna (Marea Renee Stamper) — Nationality: American. Birth: 22 October 1977, rural Kentucky, United States; a securely verified city is unavailable. Her importance also includes advocacy for women, LGBTQ artists and other marginalized communities. Key songs: We Still Believe; He Is the Voice I Hear; Marea (We’ve Lost Dancing).
